A visualization workout. Read a few moves, play them **in your head** (no board!), then answer the question cards. Click a card to check yourself.

Don't try to see the whole board at once — nobody does. Add a few moves to what you can already see, then ask the simple questions:

- Where are the pawns? (The pawn structure is the landmark of the position.)
- Which pieces have moved off their first squares?
- Where are the kings?
- What is the material — who is ahead?

If the picture gets blurry, look at one corner of the board at a time, or go piece by piece. A board picture appears only every few steps so you can check yourself.

The method comes from Andy Soltis, *The Inner Game of Chess*.

## Games by length
The games are grouped by how many moves they last. Start short and work up:

- Under 15 moves
- 15–20 moves — [Morphy's Opera Game](#opera-game)
- 21–30 moves
- Over 30 moves
